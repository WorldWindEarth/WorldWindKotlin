Terrain Ray Tutorial

Shows where a camera’s frame actually lands. A camera stands off the north face of Mount St. Helens and looks at it; each corner of its frame is a ray, and Terrain.firstHitFrom follows one and answers where the ground first rises to meet it. The quad joining those answers is the footprint — what the camera can see.

The first surface wins

The rays are drawn from the camera to where they stopped. Aimed across a ridge, a ray stops on the near face rather than carrying on to the valley behind it, and the footprint is pulled in with it. A projection onto a level plane would put that corner kilometres further out, on ground the camera cannot see.

The caller chooses the resolution

FixedResolutionTerrain samples the elevation model at a spacing given to it, not the one the renderer happens to be drawing, so the answer does not change as you zoom. Switch between 30 m and 500 m and watch the footprint move: at 500 m the ridge is averaged away and the rays run past it.

Conservative cells takes each cell’s greatest sample instead of interpolating its corners, so a crest narrower than the sampling step over-occludes rather than vanishing.

Elevation data arrives asynchronously, so the first trace on cold tiles may decline — the footprint stays hidden rather than guessing. Run an action again once the terrain has drawn.
