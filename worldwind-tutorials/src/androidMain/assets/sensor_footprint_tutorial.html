Sensor Frames: 100 Drones Tutorial

A hundred drones fly loops over the ridges and valleys around Mount St. Helens, 80 to 600 m above the ground, each with a camera whose frame is drawn on the terrain as it flies. Each drone flies its own circle or figure-eight, at its own radius, speed and height, looks along its track and slowly tilts its gimbal between 10° and 60° down. The animation runs on the frame clock.

The frames are drawn by a SensorFrameOverlay, entirely on the GPU, like sun shadows with the camera as the sun: the terrain is drawn once more and every pixel inside a camera's frame is tinted, so the frames follow the hills exactly. The panel on the right switches, while the drones fly:

  • Frustum frame — the frame's outline on the ground (off by default).
  • Straight sides — with the frustum frame on, the frame is drawn instead as four paths between where the corner rays land (a FootprintSolver): the near and far edges on the ground, the left and right sides straight through the air.
  • Seen tint — ground inside the frame the drone sees (on, red at 10 %), with a colour and an opacity.
  • Hidden tint — ground inside the frame behind a ridge (off, blue at 25 %).
  • Picture — an animated test card shared by every drone, shown on the ground each drone sees in place of the seen tint; the hidden tint stays.
  • Elevation outline — each drone's viewshed over the elevation data, from a ViewshedEngine on a worker thread, its regions drawn as paths on the ground.
  • Occlusion — a small depth map rendered from each drone, like a shadow map, tells the seen ground from the hidden.
  • Drones — 1, 10 or 100.

The depth atlas holds 16 frames. With 100 drones, drones 1 to 16 are occluded and drones 17 to 100 are drawn without occlusion: their seen tint (or picture) covers their whole frame, ridges or not, and their hidden tint never shows. The panel shows the atlas size and which drones it leaves out.

The top left corner shows the frame rate, the mean and standard deviation of render and draw time, the overlay's draw calls a frame and the viewshed sweeps a second; the same is written to the log every 5 s.

Example

SensorFootprintFragment.kt

The SensorFootprintFragment class extends BasicGlobeFragment, starts a SensorFootprintTutorial against the engine and adds the control panel and the measurements over the globe.

SensorFootprintTutorial.kt (excerpt)

The SensorFootprintTutorial class flies the drones and styles their frames from the settings. Each drone carries a Sensor; one SensorFrameOverlay draws every drone's frame. Each frame, the drones move their sensors and the overlay's frames take their colours, picture and occlusion from the panel. The full source, with the viewsheds, the straight sides and the measurements, is in worldwind-tutorials/src/commonMain/kotlin/earth.worldwind.tutorials/SensorFootprintTutorial.kt, and the animated test card in TestPatternTexture.kt beside it.
